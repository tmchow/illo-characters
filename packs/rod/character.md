# Rod — character pack

A calm snake that keeps one hand on the walking stick it lives around. The
name reads off the design — the *rod* is the thing it never lets go of, and
holding on is most of the job.

Credit: **Rod by Evan Emerson**

Style: **riso**
Cutout chroma: **magenta**
Aliases: snake, serpent, asclepius, staff

## Locked design

- **Body**: a smooth, soft-bodied snake — one continuous tapering tube, thick
  behind the head and narrowing to a simple rounded tail tip. No legs, no
  arms, no fins, no scales drawn as texture. The body is a clean unbroken
  form.
- **Rod**: a plain **knotty wooden walking stick** — a slightly irregular
  vertical staff with two or three visible knots and a blunt rounded top, cut
  from a branch rather than machined. It stands upright and the snake is
  always wound around it. The rod is a locked companion object and appears in
  every render.
- **Coil**: the snake winds around the rod in a loose **two-to-three-turn
  spiral**, head emerging near the top and the tail hanging free below the
  lowest turn. The exact turn count may shift run to run; it must always read
  at a glance as a relaxed spiral around the stick, never a tight spring, a
  knot, or a single loop.
- **Face**: two small round dot eyes on the head, plus **one thin flat
  horizontal line for a mouth**, set below and between the eyes and no wider
  than the gap between them. Blank and deadpan — **no brows, no fangs, no
  tongue, no nostrils**, ever. The mouth is a straight line and never curves
  into a smile or frown.
- **Accent carrier**: the blunt **rounded top of the rod** — the only
  accent-colored part. Never the eyes, never the mouth, never the tail tip,
  never the whole rod.
- Character comes from the calm draped posture and the soft body against the
  rough stick, never from added parts.
- Never add: a second snake, wings, a bowl or cup, a cross, a star badge,
  scales as texture, a forked tongue, fangs, a hood, eyes on the rod.

## Interaction model

- Contact surfaces: the **coiled body** — Rod wraps, grips, and holds by
  winding around things (this is its one real grip, and it is a strong one);
  and the **head**, which nudges, points, and presses. It has no hands and no
  feet; it never picks anything up.
- Reach: articulated — the head and forward body can extend and curve some
  distance from the rod, but the lower coils stay wound around it.
- Grip: wrap — a coil around a shaft, branch, handle, or limb. Never a pinch
  or a finger grasp.
- Support/locomotion: the coil itself bears the weight, wound around the
  standing rod; on the ground it rests on its own body mass and glides.
- Protected regions: the face interior — only the two dot eyes and the one
  thin mouth line appear there.
- Special operators: none. The tail tip is not a hand and does not operate
  objects; the rod is held, never wielded as a tool.

## Value rules

- **Dark/bold palettes**: the snake body may read dark — filled with the
  structure ink — with paper-white dot eyes and a paper-white mouth line, and
  the wooden rod holds a mid value so the coil stays legible against it.
- **Light/warm palettes**: the snake body is light/cream with a structure-ink
  outline, and the eyes and mouth line are structure ink (never pure black).
  The wooden rod reads slightly darker than the body so the spiral separates.
  The rod top stays the accent hue in both — exactly one accent.

## Personality

Unhurried and steady, with a dry sense of its own dignity — it has been
holding this stick a long time and sees no reason to stop. It stays wrapped
around the one thing worth keeping hold of and leans out to look at whatever
comes near, curious but never startled. Good for patience, keeping watch over
something that matters, and staying attached to first principles — and, as one
lens, for long-lived systems and the things you refuse to let go of.
